Commercial Property Investment

Asset Class Fundamentals

Understanding Commercial Real Estate Evaluation

Commercial real estate differs fundamentally from residential property. Valuation is tightly coupled with tenant creditworthiness, remaining lease duration, structural asset grade, replacement cost, and micro-market vacancy trends.

This educational guide highlights the analytical frameworks utilized by institutional commercial property evaluators.

Sector Dynamics

Investment Attributes by Commercial Asset Class

Each commercial property category exhibits unique risk-return parameters and tenant behavior.

🏢

Office Towers

Characterized by long lease lock-ins (typically 3 to 9 years), corporate institutional tenants, and capital expenditure on technical fit-outs.

Key Factor: Core-to-glass depth and IT SEZ compliance.
🛍️

High-Street Retail

Driven by direct footfall density and brand visibility. Frequently structured with base minimum guarantees paired with revenue revenue-share clauses.

Key Factor: Pedestrian frontage and parking ease.
🏭

Logistics & Warehousing

Accelerated by national supply chain consolidation. High tenant stickiness due to substantial tenant automation infrastructure investments.

Key Factor: Arterial highway and freight corridor access.
🏛️

Commercial Plazas

Whole building assets offering single-entity ownership control, naming rights potential, and multi-tenant diversification capabilities.

Key Factor: Independent land title and structural longevity.
Evaluation Matrix

Commercial Property Selection Considerations

Key criteria to inspect during commercial real estate due diligence.

1. Tenant Covenant Quality

Assessing the financial balance sheet, market capitalization, and operational longevity of the leasing corporation.

2. Lease Terms & Lock-in

Examining lease duration, security deposit months, lock-in penalty enforceability, and predetermined rental escalation steps.

3. Physical Asset Specifications

Verifying structural floor load capacity, HVAC energy efficiency, parking allocation ratio, and fire safety code compliance.

4. Title & Zoning Clearances

Ensuring unencumbered commercial freehold title, sanctioned building plans, and occupancy certification (OC).

5. Micro-Market Vacancy

Reviewing absorption rates, upcoming speculative supply pipelines, and tenant retention ratios within the immediate zone.

6. Operating Expenses (CAM)

Reviewing Common Area Maintenance charges, property taxes, insurance coverage, and sinking fund allocations.
